<guidance topic="district-specific-absence">
<title>District-specific absence narration</title>
<scope>Applies when a metric is not applicable or doesn't apply because of state law, right-to-work status, collective bargaining restrictions, or another legal framework — phrase the absence as specific to the named district, not as a blanket statement about the state.</scope>

When execution determines that a metric is not applicable due to a state-level legal framework (e.g. no collective bargaining, right-to-work, state statute prohibiting a practice), narrate the absence at the **district level**, not the **state level**.

## Rule

Say: "This metric is not applicable for **[District Name]** because **[specific legal reason]**."

Do NOT say: "[State] doesn't have X" or "[State] doesn't require X" or "[State] is not a collective bargaining state."

The distinction matters because:
- A state-level framing implies the data simply doesn't exist for the whole state, which may be wrong (other districts in the same state can still be covered or not covered independently).
- A district-level framing is always true: the metric is not applicable *for this district* given the legal context.
- NCTQ tracks coverage at the district level; absence claims must match that grain.

## Examples

<example>
Situation: Atlanta Public Schools — collective bargaining metric — Georgia prohibits collective bargaining for teachers.

CORRECT: "This metric is not applicable for Atlanta Public Schools because Georgia law does not permit collective bargaining for teachers."

INCORRECT: "Georgia is not a collective bargaining state, so this metric doesn't apply." (overbroad — frames the absence as a state-wide data gap rather than a district-specific inapplicability)
</example>

<example>
Situation: Austin ISD — a metric that requires a union contract — Texas is a right-to-work state.

CORRECT: "This metric is not applicable for Austin ISD because Texas law prohibits collective bargaining agreements for public school teachers."

INCORRECT: "Texas doesn't require collective bargaining, so there's no data for this metric." (overbroad state-level framing)
</example>

<example>
Situation: Phoenix USD — a metric about a specific state-mandated policy that Arizona does not have.

CORRECT: "This metric is not applicable for Phoenix USD because Arizona does not have a state mandate for this policy."

INCORRECT: "Arizona doesn't have this policy." (state-level framing that omits the district anchor)
</example>

## When this fires

This guidance applies whenever the planner or renderer would otherwise write a sentence with the state as the subject of an absence claim (e.g. "[State] doesn't have X", "[State] is not a …", "[State] doesn't require …"). Redirect that sentence to name the district first and cite the state legal reason second.
</guidance>
