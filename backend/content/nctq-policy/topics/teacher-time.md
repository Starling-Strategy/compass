# Teacher Time

## Topic Brief

Research consistently bears out that teachers are the most important school-based influence on student outcomes, so teachers' time is immensely valuable. Planning time, when sufficient and strategically allocated, can help ensure teachers begin each day with a roadmap for successful instruction and can provide teachers the opportunity to work together and strengthen their own and each others' work. While state law establishes the minimum amount of time students must be in school each year, districts have more control over the school calendar for teachers, who generally work extra days on school sites without students present.

Pathfinder tracks:

- Planning time, including minimum weekly planning time and collaborative planning time.
- The school year, including the number of student-teacher contact days and the total workdays in the school year.

## Stances

### [stance:teacher-time-planning-collaboration-time] Planning And Collaboration Time

Teachers need adequate time to plan and to collaborate with colleagues during working hours.

### [stance:teacher-time-planning-time] Planning Time

Districts should provide teachers with adequate planning time each week, separate from time allocated for lunch or required school duties, and within their designated work hours.

### [stance:teacher-time-collaboration-time] Collaboration Time

Districts should provide teachers with regular time to collaborate with their colleagues, especially with teachers teaching the same grade or subject.

## Research Rationales

### [rationale:teacher-time-planning-time] Planning Time

- stance_id: stance:teacher-time-planning-time
- source_id: source:nctq-research-teacher-time
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/teacher-time/
- citation_status: ready

Research confirms that increased instructional time and adequate planning time are associated with improved student outcomes. Teachers need dedicated time for lesson preparation, grading, and professional development that is distinct from supervisory duties.

### [rationale:teacher-time-collaboration-time] Collaboration Time

- stance_id: stance:teacher-time-collaboration-time
- source_id: source:nctq-research-teacher-time
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/teacher-time/
- citation_status: ready

Professional learning communities and structured collaboration time foster data-driven instructional improvement. Research shows collaboration with peers improves instructional quality and teacher retention, particularly among teachers of color.
