# Benefits

## Topic Brief

Benefits, together with salary, make up the full picture of a teacher's total compensation package. Benefits generally include insurance and pension plans. States tend not to address insurance benefits in labor statutes, but they do regulate and manage teacher pension plans. Insurance benefits are either set or negotiated at the district level, and there is a broad range of benefit packages offered among districts. Most districts offer employees some choice regarding health insurance, whether among carriers, plan designs, or both. Other common fringe benefits are tuition reimbursement, which can take the form of direct reimbursement or loan forgiveness, and reimbursement of National Board Certification fees.

Pathfinder tracks:

- Health benefits.
- Tuition reimbursement.
- Resignation and retirement policies.
- Other benefits for teachers.

Pensions are not currently tracked and are therefore not included in this research summary. Research related to paid leave is covered in the Leave topic.

## Stances

### [stance:benefits-financial-physical-wellbeing] Financial And Physical Well-Being

Districts should provide benefits that support teachers' financial and physical well-being.

### [stance:benefits-health-insurance-low-cost] Health Insurance At Low Cost

Even as health-insurance rates rise, districts should continue to provide adequate health insurance at a low cost to teachers.

### [stance:benefits-tuition-reimbursement] Tuition Reimbursement

Districts facing persistent teacher shortages should consider offering tuition reimbursement to teachers in hard to staff schools and subjects, although based on the research, this approach may be less effective than offering higher pay to these teachers.

### [stance:benefits-retirement-notification] Retirement And Resignation Notification

Districts should set retirement and resignation notification deadlines so that the district has plenty of advance notice to post job openings well in advance, which can support hiring higher-quality new teachers.

### [stance:benefits-end-of-year-retirement] End-Of-Year Retirement

Districts should incentivize teachers to retire at the end of the year rather than retiring mid-year, as this is disruptive to student learning.

## Research Rationales

### [rationale:benefits-health-insurance-competitive] Health Insurance

- stance_id: stance:benefits-financial-physical-wellbeing
- source_id: source:nctq-research-benefits
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/benefits/
- citation_status: ready

About 80% of teachers say their employer paid some or all of their health insurance premiums, compared to 74% of similar working adults. Benefits are a significant component of total compensation and help districts remain competitive.

### [rationale:benefits-health-insurance-affordability] Health Insurance Affordability

- stance_id: stance:benefits-health-insurance-low-cost
- source_id: source:nctq-research-benefits
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/benefits/
- citation_status: ready

As healthcare costs rise, maintaining affordable health insurance coverage is essential for teacher recruitment and retention. Districts should balance cost containment with comprehensive coverage.

### [rationale:benefits-tuition-reimbursement] Tuition Reimbursement

- stance_id: stance:benefits-tuition-reimbursement
- source_id: source:nctq-research-benefits
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/benefits/
- citation_status: ready

While tuition reimbursement can incentivize teachers in shortage areas, research suggests direct payments such as salary increases and bonuses may be more effective than loan forgiveness or tuition programs. A Florida program offering up to $10,000 in loan repayments showed a 24.5% decrease in teacher attrition, with greater effects from direct bonuses.

### [rationale:benefits-retirement-notification] Retirement And Resignation Notification

- stance_id: stance:benefits-retirement-notification
- source_id: source:nctq-research-benefits
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/benefits/
- citation_status: ready

Advance notification of teacher departures gives districts time to conduct thorough hiring processes rather than making last-minute placements, which is particularly important for hard-to-staff positions.

### [rationale:benefits-retirement-timing] Retirement Timing

- stance_id: stance:benefits-end-of-year-retirement
- source_id: source:nctq-research-benefits
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/benefits/
- citation_status: ready

Mid-year teacher departures are particularly disruptive to student learning. Districts should create incentives that encourage teachers planning retirement to complete the school year.

- district_id:
- state: KS
- subtopic: Health benefits
- source_url: https://teacherquality.nctq.org/contract-database/district/wichita

See their policies in their Benefits handbook.

### [exemplar:jackson-affordable-higher-tier-health-plan] Jackson Public Schools (MS)

- district: Jackson Public Schools (MS)
- district_id:
- state: MS
- subtopic: Health benefits
- source_url: https://teacherquality.nctq.org/contract-database/district/jackson

While the state covers the cost of the base employee healthcare plan, Jackson Public Schools keeps premiums for the higher-tier Select plan affordable - ranging from just $20 to $50 per month. This approach helps attract and retain staff by making quality healthcare accessible and financially sustainable. View Jackson's current healthcare options.
