# Differentiated Pay

## Topic Brief

In addition to a base salary that is typically determined by years of experience and education, more districts are offering additional pay for teachers working in schools and subjects where they are most needed and for teachers who do a great job creating learning and growth for their students.

Pathfinder tracks:

- Additional pay for working in hard-to-staff schools.
- Additional pay for working in hard-to-staff subjects.
- Performance pay based on evaluation ratings or other measures of teacher effectiveness.
- Additional pay for other qualifications, such as earning National Board Certification or serving as a department chair.

## Stances

### [stance:differentiated-pay-staffing-needs] Align Pay With Staffing Needs

Districts should not pay all teachers in lockstep. Districts should pay teachers in a way that aligns with staffing needs and desired student outcomes. This often means paying teachers more for working in hard-to-staff schools and subjects.

### [stance:differentiated-pay-hard-to-staff-subjects] Hard-To-Staff Subject Pay

Districts should pay teachers more for working in hard-to-staff subjects. That pay should be substantial enough to motivate teachers to work in these subjects, which research estimates to be at least 7.5% of their salaries ($5,000) or more.

### [stance:differentiated-pay-hard-to-staff-schools] Hard-To-Staff School Pay

Districts should pay teachers more for working in hard-to-staff schools. That pay should be substantial enough to motivate teachers to work in these schools, which research estimates to be at least 7.5% of their salary ($5,000) or more.

### [stance:differentiated-pay-nbct-stipends] NBCT Stipends

Districts should assess the value of existing differentiated pay, such as stipends for National Board Certified Teachers (NBCT), and determine whether they are serving the greater purpose of supporting student outcomes, as National Board Certification may be an effective way to identify strong teachers but may not help teachers become more effective. For example, limit the NBCT stipends to teachers working in low-performing or hard-to-staff schools to motivate more effective teachers to work in those schools.

### [stance:differentiated-pay-performance-pay] Performance Pay

Districts should consider offering performance pay, especially as a strategy to retain highly effective teachers. That pay should be substantial enough to motivate teachers, which research estimates to be around $5,000 or more.

## Research Rationales

### [rationale:differentiated-pay-hard-to-staff-schools-recruit-retain] Hard-To-Staff Schools

- stance_id: stance:differentiated-pay-staffing-needs
- source_id: source:nctq-research-differentiated-pay
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/differentiated-compensation/
- citation_status: ready

Numerous studies have found that paying teachers more to work in hard-to-staff or low-performing schools is an effective strategy to both recruit and retain teachers for those schools. When incentives are targeted to more effective teachers, evidence suggests that this practice can often lead to improved student outcomes. The Dallas ACE program offers highly effective teachers a $2,000 signing bonus and annual stipends of $6,000 to $10,000 to work at low-performing schools, resulting in a much greater share of effective teachers and substantially increased student achievement.

### [rationale:differentiated-pay-hard-to-staff-subjects] Hard-To-Staff Subjects

- stance_id: stance:differentiated-pay-hard-to-staff-subjects
- source_id: source:nctq-research-differentiated-pay
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/differentiated-compensation/
- citation_status: ready

Paying teachers more for working in a hard-to-staff subject can boost recruitment and retention, especially if the additional pay is great enough to influence teachers' decisions about what certification to pursue or whether to stay in teaching. Hawaii raised all special education teachers' salaries by $10,000, which reduced the share of vacant special education positions by 32% on average and reduced unlicensed teachers by 35%. Direct payments may be more effective than loan forgiveness programs.

### [rationale:differentiated-pay-hard-to-staff-schools-incentives] Hard-To-Staff School Incentives

- stance_id: stance:differentiated-pay-hard-to-staff-schools
- source_id: source:nctq-research-differentiated-pay
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/differentiated-compensation/
- citation_status: ready

A randomized controlled trial provided $20,000 incentives for teachers to transfer to high-need schools. Results showed increased share of effective teachers transferred, higher retention rates (93% vs 70% for control), and greater elementary student achievement. In Tennessee, a $5,000 retention bonus for highly effective teachers in priority schools increased retention by about 20 percentage points.

### [rationale:differentiated-pay-nbct-stipends] NBCT Stipends

- stance_id: stance:differentiated-pay-nbct-stipends
- source_id: source:nctq-research-differentiated-pay
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/differentiated-compensation/
- citation_status: ready

National Board Certification stipends are widespread but their effectiveness depends on targeting. Research suggests limiting NBCT bonuses to teachers serving in high-need schools or subjects, where the credential's impact on student outcomes is most clearly demonstrated.

### [rationale:differentiated-pay-performance-pay] Performance Pay

- stance_id: stance:differentiated-pay-performance-pay
- source_id: source:nctq-research-differentiated-pay
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/differentiated-compensation/
- citation_status: ready

A meta-analysis of 37 studies found that teacher performance pay programs have a positive and statistically significant effect on students' test scores. Programs were more effective when they offered larger incentives above 7.5% of the local region's income, or about $5,000, and lasted longer. In DC, a performance pay system embedded within a robust evaluation system, IMPACT, offers teachers as much as $25,000 in bonuses depending on their effectiveness rating and whether they work in a high-need school.
