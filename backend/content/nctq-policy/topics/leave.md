# Leave

## Topic Brief

In addition to standard school vacations, most districts provide teachers with additional sick days and personal days each academic year, sometimes combining the two into a bank of days available for leave. Many districts have policies that allow teachers to accumulate unused leave days from year to year. In some cases, teachers may exchange some or all of the unused days for pay at the end of each school year or at retirement. The most common types of additional paid leave days include professional development, bereavement, jury duty, military service, and work-related legal proceedings or injuries. Policies allowing for paid parental leave are becoming increasingly common among both states and districts.

Pathfinder tracks:

- Sick leave, including available days and whether days can be carried over.
- Personal leave, including available days and whether days can be carried over.
- Parental leave, including available days and who is eligible.

## Stances

### [stance:leave-sick-personal-leave] Sick And Personal Leave

Teachers make the biggest impact on students when they are present in school. But being a teacher should not mean never being able to take a day off without losing pay. Teachers should have adequate access to paid sick leave and a limited amount of paid personal leave.

### [stance:leave-parental-leave] Parental Leave

Districts should provide all teachers with access to 12 weeks of paid parental leave when welcoming a child, whether through birth, adoption, or fostering. Eligibility should be extended to all teachers who become parents - including fathers, adoptive parents, and other types of non-birthing parents.

### [stance:leave-attendance-incentives] Attendance Incentives

Districts should consider policies that recognize or incentivize strong teacher attendance, such as through including teacher attendance in performance evaluations or paying for unused leave.

### [stance:leave-attendance-tracking] Attendance Tracking

Districts should track teacher attendance rates by school and teacher, provide data back to school leaders, and flag teachers who are chronically absent, apart from parental leave, medical leave, or short-/long-term disability.

## Research Rationales

### [rationale:leave-sick-personal-leave] Sick And Personal Leave

- stance_id: stance:leave-sick-personal-leave
- source_id: source:nctq-research-leave
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/leave/
- citation_status: ready

The average teacher absence is 9.4 days per year. Policies should balance adequate leave with attendance accountability. Excessive absences disrupt student learning and impose costs on districts for substitute coverage.

### [rationale:leave-parental-leave] Parental Leave

- stance_id: stance:leave-parental-leave
- source_id: source:nctq-research-leave
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/leave/
- citation_status: ready

Comprehensive parental leave policies support teacher retention and equity. Leave should be inclusive of all parents regardless of gender or biological relationship to the child. Some states, such as Delaware, now mandate 12 weeks of paid leave for all types of parents.

### [rationale:leave-attendance-incentives] Attendance Incentives

- stance_id: stance:leave-attendance-incentives
- source_id: source:nctq-research-leave
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/leave/
- citation_status: ready

Financial incentives for good attendance can reduce teacher absenteeism. Some districts, such as Brevard, allow teachers with four or fewer absences to receive 80% of their daily pay rate for up to ten unused leave days, rewarding reliability and consistency.

### [rationale:leave-attendance-tracking] Attendance Tracking

- stance_id: stance:leave-attendance-tracking
- source_id: source:nctq-research-leave
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/leave/
- citation_status: ready

Systematic attendance tracking enables districts to identify patterns and intervene appropriately. Tracking should exclude protected leave categories such as parental, medical, and disability leave to avoid penalizing teachers exercising legal rights.
