# Collective Bargaining

## Topic Brief

There is no federal law providing public sector employees the right to bargain collectively. Each state regulates the rights of both public and private sector employees to unionize and bargain collectively, whether through state statute or through decisions handed down by the courts. States define the obligation of districts to bargain and also decide which issues can be negotiated.

Pathfinder tracks:

- Whether the state permits collective bargaining.
- The topics for which collective bargaining is required, permitted, or prohibited.
- The legality of strikes.
- The names of the collective bargaining units in the district.

## Stances

### [stance:collective-bargaining-no-policy-stance] No NCTQ Policy Stance

NCTQ does not have a policy stance on state or district policies around collective bargaining or the legality of teacher strikes.

## Research Rationales

### [rationale:collective-bargaining-research-mixed] Research Is Mixed

- stance_id: stance:collective-bargaining-no-policy-stance
- source_id: source:nctq-research-collective-bargaining
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/collective-bargaining/
- citation_status: ready

Research shows mixed outcomes for unionization. Unionized districts have approximately 5% higher salaries but mixed effects on student achievement. Recent studies suggest collaborative labor-management agreements may correlate with higher test scores. States vary widely in how they regulate teacher unionization rights.
