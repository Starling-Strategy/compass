# Evaluation

## Topic Brief

Teacher evaluation systems are shaped by both state and local policy: states generally set minimum requirements, which may include evaluation frequency or required components, while districts may establish additional parameters governing how evaluations are implemented. The actual instruments used to evaluate teachers are usually decided at the district level, frequently in negotiation with the local union. Components vary from district to district, but common components include student achievement or growth data, classroom observations, and professional development plans.

Pathfinder tracks:

- Overall system, such as the number of evaluation rating options.
- Evaluators, such as who may evaluate teachers.
- Feedback and observations, such as method for feedback and time limit for providing feedback.
- Evaluation requirements for non-tenured teachers.
- Evaluation requirements for tenured teachers.
- Summative evaluation for teachers of tested subjects.
- Summative evaluation for teachers of untested subjects.
- Consequences of a negative evaluation.

Compensation tied to teacher evaluations is covered in the Differentiated Pay topic.

## Stances

### [stance:evaluation-purpose] Purpose

Districts' evaluation systems should provide a valid measure of teacher quality, promote teachers' growth over time, identify highly effective teachers to support their retention, and identify persistently ineffective teachers who need to be placed on an improvement plan or exited from the district, all in service of improved student outcomes.

### [stance:evaluation-frequency] Frequency

Districts should require that teachers be formally evaluated annually, regardless of tenure status.

### [stance:evaluation-multiple-measures] Multiple Measures

District evaluation systems should comprise multiple measures, including observations, objective measures of student growth, and student surveys.

### [stance:evaluation-rating-categories] Rating Categories

District evaluation systems should include at least three rating categories for teachers.

### [stance:evaluation-feedback] Feedback

Evaluators should provide teachers with written feedback and a post-observation conference after each observation.

### [stance:evaluation-observations] Observations

Evaluators should observe teachers multiple times as part of each evaluation cycle.

### [stance:evaluation-evaluators] Evaluators

Districts should allow a range of people to conduct observations, including school and district administrators and lead teachers.

### [stance:evaluation-evaluator-training] Evaluator Training

Districts should provide all observers adequate training to ensure that all teachers receive accurate and actionable feedback following each observation.

### [stance:evaluation-bias-monitoring] Bias Monitoring

Districts should monitor evaluation data for evidence of bias based on the demographics of the teachers being evaluated, the people conducting observations, and the students assigned to the teachers being evaluated.

### [stance:evaluation-data-use] Data Use

Districts should use evaluation information to inform decisions such as tenure, layoffs and recalls, compensation, identifying teachers for leadership opportunities such as serving as mentors or lead teachers, tailoring professional development, and incentivizing effective teachers to work in hard-to-staff schools.

## Research Rationales

### [rationale:evaluation-purpose] Purpose

- stance_id: stance:evaluation-purpose
- source_id: source:nctq-research-evaluation
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/evaluation/

Effective evaluation serves dual purposes: supporting teacher development and informing personnel decisions. Research from districts with strong evaluation systems, including Dallas, Denver, DC, and Newark, showed medium, positive, and statistically significant effects on student math and reading achievement.

### [rationale:evaluation-frequency] Frequency

- stance_id: stance:evaluation-frequency
- source_id: source:nctq-research-evaluation
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/evaluation/
- citation_status: ready

Annual evaluation ensures regular feedback and accountability for all teachers. Reducing evaluation frequency for tenured teachers can create information gaps and reduce opportunities for professional growth.

### [rationale:evaluation-multiple-measures] Multiple Measures

- stance_id: stance:evaluation-multiple-measures
- source_id: source:nctq-research-evaluation
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/evaluation/
- citation_status: ready

A comprehensive evaluation system that incorporates multiple data sources provides a more accurate and fair assessment of teacher effectiveness. Each measure captures different dimensions of teaching quality.

### [rationale:evaluation-rating-categories] Rating Categories

- stance_id: stance:evaluation-rating-categories
- source_id: source:nctq-research-evaluation
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/evaluation/
- citation_status: ready

Binary pass/fail systems provide insufficient differentiation. At least three categories, such as highly effective, effective, and ineffective, enable meaningful distinctions that support both recognition and improvement.

### [rationale:evaluation-feedback] Feedback

- stance_id: stance:evaluation-feedback
- source_id: source:nctq-research-evaluation
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/evaluation/
- citation_status: ready

Timely, specific written feedback paired with in-person discussion is essential for evaluation to drive improvement. Without feedback, observations become compliance exercises rather than development tools.

### [rationale:evaluation-observations] Observations

- stance_id: stance:evaluation-observations
- source_id: source:nctq-research-evaluation
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/evaluation/
- citation_status: ready

A single observation provides an unreliable snapshot. Multiple observations across different contexts yield a more accurate picture of teaching practice and enable evaluators to assess growth over time.

### [rationale:evaluation-evaluators] Evaluators

- stance_id: stance:evaluation-evaluators
- source_id: source:nctq-research-evaluation
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/evaluation/
- citation_status: ready

Diverse evaluator perspectives reduce individual bias and increase reliability. Lead teachers or instructional coaches can provide subject-specific feedback that administrators may not be equipped to offer.

### [rationale:evaluation-evaluator-training] Evaluator Training

- stance_id: stance:evaluation-evaluator-training
- source_id: source:nctq-research-evaluation
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/evaluation/
- citation_status: ready

Evaluator calibration and training are critical for inter-rater reliability. Without adequate training, evaluation scores can vary widely between evaluators, undermining the system's fairness and utility.

### [rationale:evaluation-bias-monitoring] Bias Monitoring

- stance_id: stance:evaluation-bias-monitoring
- source_id: source:nctq-research-evaluation
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/evaluation/
- citation_status: ready

Evaluation systems must be audited regularly for racial, gender, and other demographic biases. Disparate impact in ratings can signal systemic issues in the evaluation process itself.

### [rationale:evaluation-data-use] Data Use

- stance_id: stance:evaluation-data-use
- source_id: source:nctq-research-evaluation
- source_title: NCTQ Research
- source_url: https://www.nctq.org/district-policy-pathfinder/research-rationales/evaluation/
- citation_status: ready

Evaluation data should drive meaningful personnel decisions. Connecting evaluation to tenure, compensation, and placement decisions creates coherent incentive structures that reward effective teaching and direct talent where it is most needed.
